I · Esposizione — first theme

The city improvises.
Dispatch must conduct.

Every morning the city begins an improvisation: calls arrive like rain — Poisson-distributed, impatient, weighted by urgency. Streets change tempo mid-phrase; weather re-writes the friction of every edge. Against this, a heterogeneous fleet — ambulances, fire engines, drones, vans — must answer with hard deadlines and finite batteries. AetherGrid answers with a rolling score: calls gather for one beat (Δ = 5 s), an assignment solver couples every voice to every call on the live graph, and a learned policy moves idle units toward tomorrow's risk before it sounds.

Cadenza · the impossible number
2012 ≈ 4.09 × 1015
naive assignments for 20 vehicles × 12 calls — a score no one sight-reads. (Pure arithmetic — no simulation needed.)

The Hungarian method takes the exact optimum in O(n³) ≈ 1.7k operations — resolved between two beats of the batch window.

II · Sviluppo — the themes, and how they play together

Three motifs — and the loop that plays them

I
Motif I ♩ · motion — pricing the world
c(e,t) = ℓe·τterr·(1 + α·ρe(t))·ωwx + β·𝟙[hazarde]
ℓe is free-flow traversal time, so every cost in the system is seconds — distance, congestion and deadlines share one unit. Closure ≠ hazard: a closed edge leaves the graph (e ∉ Et); a hazard only raises its price. α = 0.85, β = 45 s.
II
Motif II ♪ · assignment — matching voice to call
min Σij cij·xij  s.t.  Σjxij ≤ 1, Σixij ≤ 1, x ∈ {0,1}
cij = ETAij + μ·max(0, t + ETAij − Dj)
The matcher never sees the map — only prices from Motif I: ETAij = distc(t)(posi, callj). Tardiness against the arrival deadline Dj is priced by μ. Leftover calls re-buffer; leftover vehicles stay idle — and become Motif III's input.
III
Motif III ♫ · foresight — arranging the idle
Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + η[ r + γ·maxa′ Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a) ]
r = −Σj wj·Tj − κ·ΔE + b·𝟙[SLA kept]
η is the learning rate — Motif I keeps α. Acts only on idle units between beats (relocate or stay, sector-level) and never overrides a live dispatch; it only arranges capacity so the next matching costs less.
Fig · one beat of the system — the motifs are not alternatives; one loop plays all three at once, every Δ = 5 s
Motion prices the world · Assignment spends that price optimally · Foresight arranges the orchestra so the next beat costs less.

III · Rondò — the stage, not the recording

Mission Control — what opening night will look like

The engine is not built yet — so there are no measurements, and we make no fulfillment promises; large-scale testing in real deployment can go awry in ways no design review can guarantee against. Instead, three hypotheses, stated before any data exists — each with the experiment that will test it and the condition that would falsify it. Left: the Phase 3 UI storyboard.
